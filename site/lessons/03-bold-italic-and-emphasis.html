Lesson 3 — Bold, Italic, and Emphasis

Small touches, big clarity. This is the shortest lesson because it's the easiest — just four patterns you'll use forever.

The four patterns

**Bold**              → **Bold**
*Italic*              → *Italic*
***Bold and italic*** → ***Bold and italic***
~~Strikethrough~~     → ~~Strikethrough~~

Rendered: Bold, Italic, Bold and italic, ~~Strikethrough~~.

You can also use underscores (_like this_), but asterisks are easier to see and type on most keyboards. Pick one style and stick with it.

Where emphasis actually helps

Emphasis is not for decoration. It's a pointing finger. Use it when a reader skimming your text needs one thing to pop out:

  • Deadlines: "Submit by Friday, May 9."
  • Warnings: "Never share the backup key."
  • Key terms when teaching: a hierarchy is a folder tree.
  • ~~Done~~ items you want to keep visible but crossed out.

A habit worth copying: the "why" line

Many note-takers start documents with one bold line explaining the document's purpose. Six months later, that one line is priceless:

**Why this file exists:** Tracking our small-business gear so tax
season takes 2 hours instead of 2 days.

Small trap, easy fix

If bolding "doesn't work," the usual culprit is a space inside the marks: ** bold ** renders literally, but **bold** works. The marks must hug the text tight, with no spaces inside.

Common failure:

** Good **   → renders with asterisks showing
**Good**     → renders as bold

And don't accidentally double-space between marks and text. Markdown parsers at GitHub and most apps agree on this, so fix it once and it fixes everywhere.

Try it now

  1. In notes.md, bold one deadline.
  2. Italicize one term you're learning.
  3. Strikethrough one item you already finished.
