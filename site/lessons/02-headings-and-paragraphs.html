Lesson 2 — Headings and Paragraphs: Giving Notes Shape

Formatting isn't decoration — it's wayfinding. Headings tell your future self (and anyone you share with) what matters and where things live.

Headings: six sizes, one character

Put # (a hash) and a space before a line of text:

# Level 1 — biggest
## Level 2 — section
### Level 3 — sub-section
#### Level 4
##### Level 5
###### Level 6 — smallest

Renders like:

Level 1 — biggest

Level 2 — section

Level 3 — sub-section

Three rules that save you trouble:

  1. One # heading per page. It's the document title.
  2. Don't skip levels (go ## then ###, not ## then ####).
  3. The space after # is required. #Nope is not a heading.

Paragraphs: the blank line is everything

Markdown has one paragraph rule, and it's strict:

This is one paragraph.

This is a second paragraph.

But this line
and this line
will be glued into the same paragraph.

Why? Markdown is built from plain text. A single line break is invisible to the reader; a blank line is the only way to start a new paragraph.

The fix for the glued-lines problem: end the previous line with two spaces, or (in most apps) a backslash \. Better yet, don't fight it — just write one idea per paragraph and use blank lines generously.

Line-by-line lists of thoughts? Use lists

If you find yourself using two-space line breaks for a "list of things," that's a sign it should be an actual list:

- Monday: call the clinic
- Tuesday: refill prescription
- Wednesday: water the plants

You'll meet lists properly in Lesson 4 — this is just a preview.

Real life example: a jump-rope routine

# Jump-Rope Plan

**Goal:** 5 minutes unbroken by June.

## This week

Mon and Wed: 3 sets of 60 seconds, rest 60 seconds between.

Fri: try 90 seconds straight, even slowly.

## After this week

Add 30 seconds per set. Rest is allowed — slow beats nothing.

Notice: # title, ## sections, a bold goal line. That's a document anyone can follow with zero explanation.

Try it now

  1. Add a ## section to notes.md called This Week.
  2. Under it, write two short paragraphs separated by a blank line.
  3. Glue test: write two lines with no blank line between them and see them join. Add the blank line. Watch them separate.
