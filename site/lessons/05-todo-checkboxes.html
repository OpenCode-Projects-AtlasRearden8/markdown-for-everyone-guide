Lesson 5 — To-Do Checkboxes: Your New Task Manager

This is the lesson people fall in love with. A checkbox list is a to-do list you can tick off — no app purchase required.

The syntax

A bullet list where each - is followed by [ ] (a tiny empty box):

- [ ] Call pharmacy about refill
- [ ] Schedule oil change
- [x] Take out recycling
- [ ] Water the fern

On GitHub, and in Obsidian, Notion, Joplin, and many other apps, that renders as real interactive checkboxes:

  • Call pharmacy about refill
  • Schedule oil change
  • Take out recycling
  • Water the fern

[x] with a lowercase x means done. [ ] (a space between the brackets) means still open.

What makes checkboxes different from plain bullets

A bullet says "here are things." A checkbox says "here is a promise, and a record of whether I kept it." Three payoffs:

  1. The list keeps its history. Last week's list shows what got done — free evidence for reviews, or just a nice feeling.
  2. Ticking builds momentum. Even one [x] moves you forward.
  3. Open boxes stay visible. Unfinished tasks don't vanish the way they do in a day-plan note.

A low-energy pattern: the three-box day

ADHD-friendly and burnout-friendly: instead of an endless list, pick three boxes for the day. Done is done — bonus items go on tomorrow's list, not today's.

# Today — Friday

- [x] Email the landlord
- [ ] Fifteen minutes on resume (timer on)
- [ ] Refill the water pitcher

*Anything else is a gift, not an obligation.*

For long weeks, mix checkboxes with the tables you'll learn in the next lesson — Lesson 7 shows a full weekly plan built exactly that way, and the checkbox walkthrough takes you step by step.

Rules to keep your checkboxes honest

  1. Start tasks with a verb. "Call pharmacy," not "pharmacy thing."
  2. Keep boxes small. "Clean garage" is five boxes' worth of work: sort, sweep, donate, trash, shelf.
  3. Don't delete finished items — mark them [x]. Crossed-out history is the point.

Try it now

  1. Make a 3-item checkbox list for today.
  2. Mark one [x].
  3. Add one nested sub-box under a task, indented two spaces.
